During this challenge, getting by with free AI restrictions and usages was a challenge in itself. We had to keep moving from AI to AI, which caused some unique and complicated problems because our information and files weren’t all in one spot, and we had to re-explain the project to each tool. Our biggest lesson was that AI worked best when we used it to attack our design instead of just write code. That led us to the rule that the LLM never decides if a food is safe, so all the medical decisions stay in a rules engine and the AI only suggests recipes. Writing the design doc together and agreeing on an API contract first let Ethan build the backend and Aleksander build the frontend at the same time. Since we couldn’t just trust the AI, we wrote 20 unit tests and built a harness that checks every persona’s recipes for restricted items, which caught a null crash, a seed catalog where everything rated Green, and a retired model. In our final run, 5/5 recipes came from the AI and 0 restricted items were served. The biggest surprise was that setup, keys, and deployment took more time than any feature, and that AI was confidently wrong often enough that asking it for evidence worked better than asking it for code. It was a challenging and rewarding experience that we’re glad we got to have.
